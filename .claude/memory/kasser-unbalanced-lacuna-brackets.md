Kasser's quotations often open or close inside a lacuna, so a lone bracket in
a transcription is usually faithful to the print, e.g. Compléments 23b
`[[ⲡⲗⲟⲩⲙⲁ̣[ⲣⲓⲕⲟⲥ]]`, `[[ϩⲁ ⲧⲧⲓ]ⲙⲏ ... ⲛⲕⲟⲗⲗⲁⲑⲟ[ⲛ]]`, and 24a `[[ⲛ]ⲉϩⲥⲉ ...]]`.

**Why:** A bracket-balance check across the Coptic spans flags these, but the
scans show Kasser printed them so.

**How to apply:** Check the scan before reporting an unbalanced lacuna bracket
as a typo. `randy.bracketed` renders them correctly, `[[ⲗⲁⲗⲱ[. . .]]]` included.
