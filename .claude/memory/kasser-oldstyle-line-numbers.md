In Kasser's Compléments, a citation such as `(BIFAO 49,11)` or
`(ManiK:70,19)` sets the line number after the comma in old-style (text)
figures. 0, 1 and 2 sit at x-height and look like subscripts, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9
descend, and 6 and 8 ascend. The page number before the comma uses lining
figures. Real subscripts do occur, but only in dialect sigla (`S` with
subscript `vl`, `A²` with subscript `o`) and in manuscript numbers (`M₈`).

**Why:** Read digit by digit, the small figures look like subscripts. Randy's
partial transcription made this mistake, rendering `ManiK:205,32` as
`205,3<sub>2</sub>`.

**How to apply:** Transcribe these line numbers as plain digits, and keep
subscript markup for sigla and manuscript numbers. Before marking a digit as a
subscript, check the whole number in the scan.
